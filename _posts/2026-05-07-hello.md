Most of what I work on is invisible by design. A radio that hands off cleanly between cells, a control plane that survives a vendor swap, a testbed that keeps running on a Friday afternoon when nobody is paying close attention — these are good outcomes precisely because no one notices. That is fine for the network, but it makes for poor writing material if I let it.

So I wanted a place to do the opposite: to stop and describe what we built, what we learned, what we got wrong, and what came of it. Not as papers — papers are for the things that survive review and end up in a venue — but as the longer, looser version of the work. The kind of thing I would have wanted to read when I was earlier in my career, trying to figure out whether wireless systems were the right thing to bet a decade on.

A few topics I expect to write about, when there is something honest to say:

- Field deployment notes from ARA, ACCoRD, and ArMORED — what actually breaks in rural environments, and what we did about it.
- Open RAN integration problems that you don't see until you have multiple vendors in the same rack.
- Observations about wireless from the angle of someone who spent years operating networks before researching them.
- Occasional notes on rural broadband policy, since I think the policy and the technology have to move together for any of this to matter.

If you're reading this and any of it sounds useful, the contact links on the [home page](/) all reach me. I'd rather have one good conversation than a hundred page views.
